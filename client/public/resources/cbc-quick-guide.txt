WHAT IS THE COMPETENCY-BASED CURRICULUM (CBC)?
The CBC focuses on what learners can do, not only what they can repeat. A
competency combines knowledge, skills, and attitudes that learners use in real
situations. Lessons should help learners apply ideas, solve problems, work with
others, and communicate clearly.

THREE QUESTIONS FOR EVERY LESSON
1. What competency am I developing?
2. What will learners be able to do at the end?
3. How will I know they can do it?

PLANNING WITH COMPETENCIES
- Start from the curriculum outcome, then design the task.
- Use real-life contexts from the learners' community.
- Give learners time to practise, not only listen.
- Plan for mixed ability: support, core task, extension.
- Include group work that requires every learner to contribute.
- Assess through observation and performance, not only tests.

ACTIVE LEARNING STRATEGIES
Think-Pair-Share
  Learners think alone, discuss with a partner, then share with the class.

Think Aloud
  The teacher models the thinking process while solving a problem.

Group Investigation
  Small groups explore a question, prepare findings, and present them.

Role Play
  Learners act out a real situation to practise language and reasoning.

Exit Ticket
  Each learner writes one answer or question before leaving.

Gallery Walk
  Groups display work and others move around giving feedback.

ASSESSMENT THAT MATCHES CBC
Use a simple rubric with three levels:
  Beginning - needs support to complete the task.
  Developing - completes the task with some help.
  Secure - completes the task independently and explains the thinking.

Record evidence such as:
  - a short observation note,
  - a sample of learner work,
  - a checklist of skills observed,
  - a learner's own reflection.

COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
- Turning a group activity into one learner doing all the work.
- Asking only recall questions.
- Giving a task without clear success criteria.
- Ignoring learners who are quiet but confused.
- Assessing only at the end instead of throughout the lesson.

QUICK CHECKLIST
[ ] The outcome describes what learners can do.
[ ] The main task is active and learner-centred.
[ ] Materials are ready before the lesson.
[ ] Support and extension are planned.
[ ] Assessment evidence is identified.
[ ] Reflection time is included.
