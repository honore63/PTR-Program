PTR ALUMNI NETWORK - INCLUSIVE EDUCATION STRATEGIES
Primary Teacher Residency Program (PTRP) | Inspire, Educate and Empower

PURPOSE
Inclusive education means every learner can take part, learn, and belong. It is
not a separate programme for a few learners. It is how we plan everyday lessons.

START WITH BELIEFS
- All learners can learn with the right support.
- Difference is normal in every classroom.
- Support is a change to teaching, not a label for a child.
- Learners should not be separated or embarrassed because they need help.

FOUR WAYS TO ADAPT A LESSON
1. Content - what learners are learning.
   Simplify the task, keep the same big idea, or provide a worked example.

2. Process - how learners learn.
   Use pictures, objects, partner talk, movement, or step-by-step instructions.

3. Product - how learners show learning.
   Allow oral answers, drawings, role play, or a shorter written response.

4. Environment - where and with whom learners learn.
   Adjust seating, lighting, noise, group size, and access to materials.

PRACTICAL STRATEGIES
For learners who struggle with reading:
- Provide a short summary with key words.
- Read instructions aloud.
- Use paired reading.
- Allow extra time.

For learners who struggle with writing:
- Accept a labelled diagram or oral explanation.
- Provide sentence starters.
- Reduce copying from the board.
- Focus feedback on ideas before handwriting.

For learners with attention difficulties:
- Give one instruction at a time.
- Use a visible schedule.
- Seat near a positive peer.
- Build in short movement breaks.

For learners with hearing needs:
- Face the learner when speaking.
- Speak clearly, not loudly.
- Use gestures and written key words.
- Reduce background noise.
- Check understanding privately.

For learners with visual needs:
- Seat near the board.
- Use large, high-contrast writing.
- Describe what you draw.
- Provide materials in a format the learner can access.

For learners learning in an additional language:
- Pre-teach key vocabulary.
- Allow thinking time.
- Let learners discuss in their first language before answering in English.
- Use visuals and demonstrations.

GROUP WORK THAT INCLUDES EVERYONE
- Give each learner a clear role.
- Keep groups small.
- Assign roles that match strengths, not only academic level.
- Rotate roles so no learner is always the helper or always the dependent.
- Observe groups and support quietly.

LANGUAGE MATTERS
Avoid:
  "This child is slow."
  "That group cannot cope."

Prefer:
  "This learner needs a visual model."
  "This group needs one instruction at a time."

FEEDBACK AND PRAISE
- Name the effort or strategy: "You checked your work before answering."
- Avoid comparing learners publicly.
- Give private, specific feedback where needed.

WORKING WITH FAMILIES
- Ask families what helps their child at home.
- Share one clear goal at a time.
- Listen without blame.
- Agree on a simple way to check progress.

REFLECTION QUESTIONS
  Who did not participate today?
  What barrier got in the way?
  What small change could remove it?
  Which learner needs a different route to the same outcome?
