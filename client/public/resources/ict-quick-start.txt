PTR ALUMNI NETWORK - ICT INTEGRATION QUICK START GUIDE
Primary Teacher Residency Program (PTRP) | Inspire, Educate and Empower

PURPOSE
Use technology only when it improves learning. A simple, reliable tool used well
is better than a complicated tool that fails in class.

START WITH THE LEARNING, NOT THE DEVICE
Ask:
1. What are learners struggling to understand?
2. How can technology help them see, practise, or explain it?
3. What will I do if the technology does not work?

LOW-RESOURCE IDEAS THAT WORK
- One phone, many learners: project or pass the phone to show a short video.
- Audio recording: learners record and listen to their own English speaking.
- Camera: photograph learner work and display it for discussion.
- Calculator: check reasoning after learners solve by hand.
- Offline video or saved images: prepare before the lesson.
- Shared document: type a class story together if a computer is available.

PLANNING CHECKLIST
[ ] The tool supports a clear learning outcome.
[ ] The activity works with the number of devices available.
[ ] Learners know what to do before devices are switched on.
[ ] A no-device backup plan is ready.
[ ] Files are downloaded in advance if the internet is unreliable.
[ ] Learners know the rules for safe and respectful use.

CLASSROOM RULES FOR SHARED DEVICES
- Handle devices with clean, dry hands.
- One learner operates at a time; the group decides what to record.
- Keep the device flat and away from the edge of the desk.
- Do not photograph learners without permission.
- Return the device to the same safe place after use.

USING VIDEO WELL
Before: tell learners what to look for.
During: pause at key moments and ask a question.
After: learners retell, discuss, or write two sentences.
Avoid playing a long video without a task.

USING AUDIO FOR ENGLISH
1. Learners rehearse a short sentence with a partner.
2. Record one learner at a time.
3. Play it back and ask: "What was clear? What could improve?"
4. Record again after feedback.
Keep recordings short and delete them when no longer needed.

SAFE AND ETHICAL USE
- Get consent before sharing photos or recordings of learners.
- Never publish a learner's full name with a photo without approval.
- Keep passwords private and lock the device when not in use.
- Do not open unknown links or install unapproved apps.
- Report damaged or missing equipment immediately.

WHEN TECHNOLOGY IS NOT AVAILABLE
Use the same teaching idea with simple materials:
- Video becomes a teacher-drawn diagram.
- Audio recording becomes peer repetition.
- Online research becomes a prepared text or community interview.
- Digital quiz becomes cards, hand signals, or a chalkboard game.

REFLECTION
After the lesson, note:
  What did the technology add?
  What distracted learners?
  Was every learner involved?
  What would I simplify next time?
