PURPOSE
This guide offers practical ways to build learners' English confidence without
slowing down subject learning. English is a tool for thinking and communicating,
not only a subject to be tested.

CREATE A SAFE LANGUAGE ENVIRONMENT
- Praise attempts before correcting accuracy.
- Let learners rehearse an answer with a partner before speaking to the class.
- Use consistent classroom phrases every day.
- Accept short answers, then gently model a fuller sentence.
- Never shame a learner for using their first language to explain an idea.

USEFUL CLASSROOM PHRASES
  "Can you say that again, please?"
  "What do you notice?"
  "Can you explain your answer?"
  "Do you agree or disagree? Why?"
  "Work with your partner for two minutes."
  "Show me with your hand if you understand."
  "Let us try one more example together."

FOUR ROUTINES THAT BUILD CONFIDENCE
1. Word Wall
   Add five subject words each week. Use them in sentences during lessons.

2. Sentence Starters
   Display starters such as "I think... because...", "First..., then...", and
   "The evidence is..." so learners can begin extended answers.

3. Pair Rehearsal
   Give thinking time, then a 30-second partner rehearsal, then whole-class share.

4. Repeat and Extend
   A learner gives a short answer. The teacher repeats it correctly, then asks
   another learner to extend it.

TEACHING VOCABULARY
- Introduce the word in context, not as an isolated list.
- Say it, clap the syllables, and use it in a sentence.
- Show a picture or real object where possible.
- Ask learners to use the word in their own example.
- Revisit the word across the week.

READING IN ENGLISH
Before reading: predict from the title and pictures.
During reading: pause to check meaning and ask "What does this word mean here?"
After reading: retell, summarise, or answer an inference question.

WRITING IN ENGLISH
- Model the sentence or paragraph first.
- Provide a short checklist of success criteria.
- Let learners draft, then improve one sentence with a partner.
- Display a few strong examples with permission.

FEEDBACK THAT HELPS
Instead of "Good", say:
  "You used 'because' correctly to explain your reason."
  "Your sentence is clear. Now add one detail about where it happened."
  "Try saying that again with a full sentence."

WORKING WITH LARGE CLASSES
- Use choral repetition for new phrases.
- Use pair work so every learner speaks.
- Choose a few learners to share rather than waiting for everyone.
- Use a consistent signal to stop and refocus.
