PTR ALUMNI NETWORK - MENTOR TEACHER HANDBOOK
Primary Teacher Residency Program (PTRP) | Inspire, Educate and Empower

PURPOSE
This handbook supports mentors who guide resident teachers or early-career
teachers. Mentoring is a professional relationship built on trust, observation,
honest feedback, and shared planning.

THE MENTOR'S ROLE
- Welcome the resident into the school community.
- Model strong teaching and explain the thinking behind it.
- Observe lessons and give specific, usable feedback.
- Co-plan lessons and help solve classroom problems.
- Protect time for reflection.
- Encourage the resident to try ideas independently.
- Communicate concerns early and respectfully.

WHAT GOOD MENTORING LOOKS LIKE
  Collaborative, not controlling.
  Specific, not vague.
  Based on evidence, not personality.
  Focused on learner progress.
  Confidential and professional.

THE MENTORING CYCLE
1. Pre-lesson conversation (10-15 minutes)
   What is the learning outcome?
   What will learners do?
   What might be difficult?
   What would you like me to observe?

2. Observation
   Record evidence: time, learner actions, teacher moves, questions asked,
   and examples of learning. Avoid writing judgements only.

3. Feedback conversation (15-20 minutes)
   Start with the resident's reflection.
   Share one strength with evidence.
   Share one priority for improvement.
   Agree on a specific next step.

4. Follow-up
   Co-plan, model, or observe again. Check the agreed next step.

GIVING FEEDBACK
Use the "strength - evidence - next step" structure:
  "The pair discussion gave every learner a chance to speak. I saw all groups
  talking for two minutes. Next time, try choosing two learners to share so the
  whole class hears the ideas."

Avoid:
  "Your lesson was good."
  "The learners were noisy."
  "You need to improve classroom management."

Replace with:
  "Learners were noisy during the transition. A countdown and a clear
  instruction might reduce the lost time."

QUESTIONS THAT BUILD REFLECTION
  What went as you expected?
  What surprised you?
  Which learners were most engaged? Why?
  Who did not participate, and what might explain it?
  What would you change if you taught this again?
  What do you want help with next?

WHEN PROGRESS IS SLOW
- Focus on one change at a time.
- Model the strategy in a short demonstration.
- Practise the strategy outside a full lesson.
- Break the task into smaller steps.
- Agree on a simple observation checklist.
- Involve school leadership only when necessary and with the resident's knowledge.

MENTOR SELF-REFLECTION
  Did I listen before advising?
  Was my feedback specific and evidence-based?
  Did I protect confidentiality?
  Did I model what I asked for?
  Did I notice progress and name it?

RECORD KEEPING
Keep brief notes:
  Date | Focus | Evidence | Agreed next step | Follow-up date
Share notes with the resident. Do not record personal or sensitive information
that is not relevant to professional growth.

CLOSING THOUGHT
A strong mentor builds a teacher's confidence and judgement. The goal is not to
create a copy of yourself, but to help the resident become the best teacher they
can be.
